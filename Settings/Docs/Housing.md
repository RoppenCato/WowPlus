# A room of your own

**Status: design only, 2026-10-11. Nothing here is built.**

Robin: "Would be cool if you could purchase a room in different cities and things could happen there. You could teleport there, have storage and other things."

The game has no housing. This page works out what can be done with what the game does have, what would be fun, and an order to build it in.

## The idea in one paragraph

You buy the key to a room in a city: a real room that already exists in the game world, upstairs in an inn or in a quiet building. It becomes a place on your hearthstone menu. In it you have storage, a mailbox, a bed that leaves you well rested, and the things you choose to put there. Your companions and guild mates drop by, trophies from your adventures appear on the walls, and now and then something happens: a letter under the door, a visitor, a stray cat. Each city's room has its own character and its own small advantage, and you can own more than one.

## What the game lets us do

| Wanted | How it can be done | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| A private room | Use rooms that already exist in the world. The furniture and visitors we add are only put there while you are inside, and taken away when you leave. | We cannot build new rooms or change walls. The room is where the game put it. |
| Buying it | The innkeeper sells a key (a real item), or it is a quest reward. Owning it is remembered per character. | None. |
| Teleport | A line in the hearthstone menu for each room you own. | None. |
| Storage | A chest that opens your own bank, and a second one that opens your guild's vault. Both are things the server can open from any object. | Extra storage beyond bank and guild vault would need a table of our own and an addon window. |
| Mail | A mailbox object in the room. | None. |
| Furniture | The game has hundreds of placeable objects: beds, chairs you can sit on, tables, rugs, braziers, bookshelves, banners, mounted heads, barrels, candles. | See "Placing things" below. |
| Crafting at home | A cooking fire, a forge, an anvil and an alchemy bench are objects that professions check for. Put one in the room and you can craft there. | None. |
| People in the room | Bots can be brought in and made to sit, eat, talk. NPCs of our own can live there. | Bots walk badly in tight rooms; they should mostly sit. |
| Things happening | The server decides from the world seed and the week, as it does for vendors and weather. | Each kind of event has to be written once. |

### Placing things

Three ways, from simple to rich:

1. **Sets.** The room comes in stages: bare, furnished, comfortable, grand. Each stage is a layout made by hand for that room. Simple and always looks right.
2. **Slots.** The room has fixed spots (wall above the bed, corner by the window, the table). You choose what goes in each from what you own.
3. **Free placement.** You stand where the thing should go and place it; an addon lets you nudge and turn it. The most fun, and the most work: things end up inside walls, and every room needs testing.

Suggested: start with sets, add slots for trophies and a few favourite pieces, and leave free placement for later.

## What a room gives you

**From the first day**

- A place on the hearthstone menu.
- A chest for your bank and one for the guild vault.
- A mailbox.
- A bed. Lying down for a moment gives "Well rested": rested experience fills quickly, and a small bonus for an hour (a little more health, or faster sprint recovery).
- A fireplace that is lit when it is night or raining outside.

**As you furnish it**

- A cooking fire, then an anvil and forge, an alchemy bench, an enchanter's table.
- A training dummy.
- A bookshelf that opens your journal.
- A map table that lists this week's bounties near that city.
- A wardrobe or a weapon rack that shows pieces you want to keep.
- A music box.
- A basket for a companion pet you own, which then wanders the room.

**Trophies, which you cannot buy**

- A champion's or rare's head on the wall after the kill.
- A banner for each dungeon cleared, a bigger one for a raid.
- A shelf of oddities: the strangest thing a vendor sold you, the first epic you found.
- These come from the journal: what it records is what can be shown.

## Things that happen there

Decided by the world seed and the week, so not even the one who built it knows what comes.

**People**

- **Companions live there.** When they are not travelling with you they are in your room: sitting by the fire, asleep at night by their own clock, cooking.
- **Guild mates drop by.** One knocks, comes in, sits and talks for a while. Chat in the room is slower and longer than in the field: an evening by the fire.
- **A neighbour.** Each city's room has one: an NPC with a character of their own who comments on your comings and goings, asks small favours, and gossips about the city.
- **Dinner.** Cook something and whoever is in the room eats with you. Everyone leaves well fed, and someone tells a story or passes on a rumour.
- **A traveller** asks to sleep on the floor for a night and leaves a gift, a map or a tall tale.

**Small events**

- A letter under the door.
- A housewarming gift from a guild mate: a piece of furniture, sent by mail.
- A stray cat that keeps coming back and, fed three times, stays.
- A leak in the roof during a storm, a mouse in the pantry.
- Something missing from the shelf, and a trail that leads into the city: a small quest.
- Decorations that appear on their own for the game's holidays.
- A wandering merchant who knocks once a week with furniture you cannot buy elsewhere.

**The world outside the window**

- With the day passing in an hour and the weather heavier, the room is where you notice it: rain on the roof, candles lit at dusk, a cold hearth in the morning.

## A room in each city

Each has a price, a look and one advantage, so the choice means something and owning several is worth it.

| City | Character | Its advantage (examples) |
|---|---|---|
| Orgrimmar | Loud, central, above the inn in the Valley of Strength | Close to everything: the cheapest, the plainest |
| Thunder Bluff | A lodge on the high rise, quiet, open to the wind | Rested experience fills fastest |
| Undercity | A vault room in the Apothecarium | An alchemy bench from the start |
| Silvermoon | Elegant, expensive | A wardrobe and the finest furniture |
| Shattrath | Neutral ground, travellers from everywhere | More visitors and rumours |
| Dalaran | A mage's attic | A portal stone: your other rooms are one click away |
| A village (Bloodhoof, Brill, Razor Hill) | Small and cheap | A first home at level 10, a garden plot outside |

Alliance cities would get theirs the same way.

**Paying for it:** a price to buy, rising steeply with the city, plus a small weekly upkeep that the neighbour collects. This gives gold a purpose at every level. Upkeep unpaid for a month does not lose the room; the fire goes out and the visitors stop until it is paid.

## What is hard, honestly

- **Finding the rooms.** Each one has to be chosen and its coordinates taken by hand, in the game. A dozen rooms is a day's work on its own.
- **Bots in small rooms.** They path badly indoors. Sitting bots are fine; walking ones will sometimes stand in a wall.
- **Free placement.** Worth doing only once the rest is loved.
- **Extra storage.** Bank and guild vault are free to offer. A cupboard with its own slots needs its own window in an addon.

## An order to build it in

1. **One room, bare.** A key from the innkeeper in one city, the hearthstone line, the two chests, the mailbox, the bed and its "well rested". Proves the whole idea.
2. **Furnishing in stages**, and crafting stations.
3. **Trophies** from the journal.
4. **Companions and visitors**, and the evening chat.
5. **More cities**, each with its advantage, and upkeep.
6. **Events** from the seed.
7. **Slots, then free placement.**

## Open questions

1. Buy once, or rent by the week? (The page assumes buy once plus small upkeep.)
2. Which city first? (Suggested: the capital of the character being played, so Thunder Bluff or Orgrimmar for Vorak.)
3. Should furniture be bought, crafted by professions, found as loot, or all three?
4. How much should the room give in power? (Suggested: comfort and convenience only, apart from the mild "well rested".)
